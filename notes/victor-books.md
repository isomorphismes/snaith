## 1. *Topics in K-Theory* (1975)

**Citation:** Luke Hodgkin and Victor Snaith, *Topics in K-Theory: Two Independent Contributions*, Lecture Notes in Mathematics **496**, Springer, 1975. [Publisher](https://link.springer.com/book/10.1007/BFb0082284). Jardine bibliography item **14** for Snaith's portion.

**Attribution:** Not a single co-written treatise. Hodgkin wrote “The equivariant Künneth theorem in K-theory,” pp. 1–101; Snaith wrote “Dyer–Lashof operations in K-theory,” pp. 103–294. Credit the two independently.

**Mathematical note.** Dyer–Lashof operations are the homology operations associated with the higher coherence of iterated loop spaces and infinite loop spaces. This contribution explores analogues in K-theory, in which the formal operations are constrained by complex K-theory's periodicity and multiplicative structure. An initial dependency chain is:
1. Understand K-homology and the external product;
2. understand the extended power construction and symmetric-group actions;
3. relate operations in homology to operations in K-theory;
4. identify integral, mod-prime, and Bockstein distinctions.

**Reading caution:** The 2023 obituary notes later corrections and technical issues in portions of these calculations, especially at odd primes. Treat original formulae as historically important, not automatically definitive. **To verify in text:** exact operation identities, hypotheses, and scope of later corrections.

## 2. *Algebraic Cobordism and K-Theory* (1979)

**Citation:** Victor P. Snaith, *Algebraic Cobordism and K-Theory*, *Memoirs of the American Mathematical Society* **21**, no. **221**, 1979, vii + 152 pp. [Freely accessible author's work PDF mirrored at Rochester](https://people.math.rochester.edu/faculty/doug/otherpapers/Snaith-cobordism.pdf).

**Core thesis.** Relate stable splittings of classifying spaces into Thom-space pieces to multiplicative generalized cohomology theories, and relate complex K-theory and cobordism to localizations of suitable stable homotopy rings.

**Key objects:** stable homotopy of a suspension spectrum; Thom spaces of universal bundles; Grassmannians and classifying spaces (notably BU and related spaces); MU (complex cobordism); the Bott element; localization or periodization with respect to a class.

**Mathematical sketch.** A stabilized classifying space carries considerably more information than an ordinary point-set model; its stable decomposition gives access to a direct-sum family of Thom-space summands. Multiplicative maps from such spectra and inversion of a Bott-like class connect apparently different cohomology theories. This is related to the celebrated *Snaith theorem* expressing complex K-theory through Bott localization of the suspension spectrum of CP∞, with conventions and suspension degrees important. The monograph also addresses analogous algebraic constructions for rings.

**Why this matters:** one can formulate “make an invariant periodic” as a universal operation in a category of spectra. But **do not** replace geometric proofs with the false assertion that a point-set classifying space *is* complex K-theory.

**Questions for a close reading:** What exact stable splitting appears in each classical family? Which localization classes are used, and with what grading? Which algebraic cobordism construction is intended in 1979, and how does it compare with later motivic MGL/KGL? How much is proved for general rings?

## 3. *Algebraic K-Theory and Localised Stable Homotopy Theory* (1983)

**Citation:** Victor P. Snaith, *Algebraic K-Theory and Localised Stable Homotopy Theory*, *Memoirs AMS* **43**, no. **280**, 1983, xi + 102 pp. [AMS](https://www.ams.org/books/memo/0280/).

**Main mathematical move:** Invert suitable Bott elements, linking algebraic K-theory of rings/fields to localized stable homotopy constructions. The localization is the *construction*, not just a post hoc computational shortcut.

**Distinguish:** topological KU/KU-homology, Quillen algebraic K-groups K_i(R), and étale/Bott-inverted variants are distinct entities. A comparison theorem may require finite coefficients, completion, inversion of a prime or roots of unity, or a field hypothesis. The book should be read with those qualifiers visible.

**Reading objectives:** locate definition of the periodic algebraic K-theory object; record the Bott class and degree; state hypotheses on R and primes; record which map is constructed and which is only studied after localization; list K-group calculations/bounds. Compare with later Quillen–Lichtenbaum-style theorems, noting the chronology.

## 5. *Topological Methods in Galois Representation Theory* (1989)

**Citation:** Victor P. Snaith, *Topological Methods in Galois Representation Theory*, Canadian Mathematical Society Series of Monographs and Advanced Texts, Wiley, 1989; later Dover edition 2014.

**Chapter progression** (based on publisher contents): abelian and nonabelian group cohomology; characteristic classes of forms and algebras; higher characteristic classes of symmetric bilinear forms and of Galois representations; stable homotopy and induced representations; explicit Brauer induction; Artin and local root numbers.

**Central bridge.** A representation of an absolute or finite Galois group is algebra, but characteristic classes send it to cohomological invariants whose behavior under sum, product, induction, restriction, and Galois descent can be studied topologically. Compare characteristic classes of orthogonal representations and symmetric bilinear forms, paying attention to Stiefel–Whitney and higher classes and their coefficient rings.

**Examples worth calculating first:** a one-dimensional character; a quadratic extension and its associated orthogonal character; a representation induced from a subgroup; determinant/discriminant and low-degree classes.

**Check while reading:** where do local/global fields enter? Which identities concern arbitrary groups, and which require orthogonality or number-theoretic hypotheses? What is the precise topology-to-epsilon-factor chain of reasoning?

## 7. *Galois Module Structure* (1994)

**Citation:** Victor P. Snaith, *Galois Module Structure*, Fields Institute Monographs **2**, American Mathematical Society, 1994 (2012 reissue). [AMS search/catalog](https://www.ams.org/books/fim/002/).

**Problem:** If L/K is a finite Galois extension with group G, objects such as O_L, units, or modules built from local/global arithmetic carry a G-action. They are modules over Z[G] (or over other relevant group rings), often failing to be free or to admit a normal integral basis. Describe/measure their structure by classes in appropriate projective/class groups.

**Structural themes:** group-ring modules; local freeness and stable equivalence; Galois cohomology; characteristic classes, induction, and root numbers. Connect to Fröhlich's and Chinburg's conjectures; verify exact emphasis in book instead of importing conclusions from similarly titled works.

**Small example:** L/K tamely ramified vs wildly ramified: compare whether O_L is a free rank-one Z[G]-module. In the wild case the normal integral basis intuition can fail. Explain exactly which hypotheses ensure an integral normal basis; this is subtler than the normal basis theorem over K[G].

**Reading deliverables:** definitions of locally free class group Cl(Z[G]); which maps of class groups are used; statement of the specific Chinburg conjectures discussed; calculations in cyclic and quaternionic examples.

## 8. *Explicit Brauer Induction: With Applications to Algebra and Number Theory* (1994)

**Citation:** Victor P. Snaith, Cambridge Studies in Advanced Mathematics **40**, Cambridge University Press, 1994 (2010 edition). [Publisher contents](https://www.cambridge.org/core/books/explicit-brauer-induction/contents/94760409B10F428267522A3BE345A2C3).

**Actual chapters:** 1. Representations. 2. Induction theorems. 3. GL₂(F_q). 4. The class-group of a group-ring. 5. A class-group miscellany. 6. Complete discrete valuation fields. 7. Galois module structure.

**Mathematical core:** Brauer induction expresses suitable characters of a finite group as integral combinations of characters induced from one-dimensional characters of subgroups. In symbols, schematically,
\[
\chi=\sum_i n_i\,\operatorname{Ind}_{H_i}^{G}\lambda_i,\qquad n_i\in\mathbb Z.
\]
This is a **character identity**, not generally an isomorphism of actual direct sums of modules, since some coefficients are negative.

Snaith's “explicit” aspect is a constructive, controlled form useful in arithmetic. The book develops the relation between induction formulae, representation rings, group-ring class groups, and local root-number questions.

**Worked cases to seek in book:** C_m, S₃, quaternion groups, GL₂(F_q). Compare Artin induction (rational coefficients, different subgroup restrictions) with Brauer induction (integral character identity via one-dimensional inducing characters). Distinguish the construction from Boltje's canonical Brauer induction formula, a major neighboring development credited in the obituary.

**Open verification questions:** canonical vs noncanonical choices; functoriality; explicit formulas for local epsilon factors; how class-group logarithms are evaluated.

## 9. *Groups, Rings and Galois Theory* (1998; second edition 2003)

**Citation:** Victor P. Snaith, *Groups, Rings and Galois Theory*, World Scientific, first edition 1998, second edition 2003. [World Scientific title search](https://www.worldscientific.com/search?query=Groups%20Rings%20and%20Galois%20Theory%20Snaith).

**Pedagogical layer:** finite groups and their structure, Sylow theorems, permutations, abelian groups (including examples involving coding/finite combinatorics); rings, factorization, fields and Galois groups; solvability of polynomials by radicals; algebraic structure of field extensions.

**Second-edition scope:** more systematic rings and modules, modules over principal ideal domains, tensor products, and Dedekind domains/ideal factorization. A two-edition comparison is needed before attributing material to the first edition.

**Candidate exercises:** explicitly compute Gal(Q(√d)/Q); finite fields and their cyclic multiplicative groups; a quartic with a known resolvent; show how solvable subgroups relate to radical solvability; examine nonprincipal ideals in a quadratic number field.

**Place in the program:** this is a readable base for the later arithmetic books; do not begin with the derived-category book if the subgroup induction and Galois module conventions are not yet fixed.

## 10. *The Second Chinburg Conjecture for Quaternion Fields* (2000)

**Citation:** **Jeffrey Hooper, Victor P. Snaith, Minh Van Tran**, *The Second Chinburg Conjecture for Quaternion Fields*, *Memoirs AMS* no. **704**, 2000, approximately 133 pages. [AMS](https://www.ams.org/books/memo/0704/).

**Joint authorship matters.** The three authors should each be credited. The result concerns number fields whose Galois group is quaternionic.

**Problem and result (abstract level):** relate a Galois module invariant from arithmetic of field extensions to root-number information attached to symplectic characters. The memoir establishes the second Chinburg conjecture in the quaternion-field setting.

**Dependencies:** quaternion group Q₈ and its irreducible (notably symplectic) representation; integral group rings; root numbers in functional equations; locally free class groups; root-number class vs Chinburg's arithmetic class.

**Caution:** “for quaternion fields” is a hypothesis, not a proof for all Galois extensions. Record the precise variant of the Chinburg conjecture, the arithmetic base field assumptions and the class-group equality when full text is read.

## 11. *Algebraic K-Groups as Galois Modules* (2002)

**Citation:** Victor P. Snaith, *Algebraic K-Groups as Galois Modules*, Progress in Mathematics **206**, Birkhäuser, 2002. [Publisher full chapter list](https://link.springer.com/book/10.1007/978-3-0348-8207-1).

**Verified contents (seven chapters):**
1. Galois Actions and L-values (pp. 1–12)
2. K-groups and Class-groups (13–57)
3. Higher K-theory of Local Fields (59–82)
4. Positive Characteristic (83–159)
5. Higher K-theory of Algebraic Integers (161–234)
6. The Wiles Unit (235–260)
7. Annihilators (261–296)

**Central question:** Higher algebraic K-groups can have nontrivial Galois-module structure. What invariants of the resulting modules generalize the traditional class-group-valued obstructions for units/integer rings, and how are they related to special values of L-functions?

**Conceptual distinction:** The arithmetic module K_i(O_L) with an induced action of Gal(L/K) is not the same thing as equivariant K-theory K^G_i(X); the former begins with a Galois action on a number ring.

**Compare:** local field vs global number field; odd/even higher K groups; positive characteristic vs characteristic zero; annihilators of relevant class groups; the relation of Wiles's theorem/units to K-theoretic classes.

**Suggested calculation:** specify G, K, L and i, compute torsion/rank of K_i(O_L) where possible, then ask for its Z[G]-module class. Mere abstract group structure is insufficient.

## 12. *Stable Homotopy Around the Arf-Kervaire Invariant* (2009)

**Citation:** Victor P. Snaith, *Stable Homotopy Around the Arf-Kervaire Invariant*, Progress in Mathematics **273**, Birkhäuser, 2009. [Publisher](https://link.springer.com/search?query=Stable+Homotopy+Around+the+Arf-Kervaire+Invariant).

**Objects:** framed manifolds and Pontryagin–Thom; stable stems π_*^S; quadratic refinements and the Arf invariant over F₂; Brown–Peterson homology, the Hurewicz map, power operations, and interactions with real projective spaces.

**Conceptual starting point:** a closed framed manifold represents a stable homotopy class; in suitable dimensions an associated quadratic form determines a mod-2 Arf–Kervaire invariant. The possible existence of framed manifolds with invariant one translates into the Kervaire invariant one problem. Do not conflate this with the ordinary Arf invariant of an arbitrary binary quadratic form.

**Book organization (publisher description):** preliminaries; detection via QX and infinite loop methods; upper-triangular/matrix techniques; a K-theoretic interlude; projective-space constructions; BP and Hurewicz discussion; upper-triangular approaches; perspectives/open problems.

**Chronology warning:** the book appeared in **2009**, before the Hill–Hopkins–Ravenel nonexistence theorem published in 2016. Its outlook and questions must be read historically. It does not constitute the modern solution to the entire problem.

**Precise next reading:** identify the upper-triangular matrix in the book (including a construction involving 1 ∧ ψ³), and sort statements proved, conditional and proposed. Match each stable dimension with Kervaire's 2^{j+1}−2 dimension pattern, with remaining exceptional-dimension caveats checked against later literature.

## 13. *Derived Langlands: Monomial Resolutions of Admissible Representations* (2018)

**Citation:** Victor P. Snaith, *Derived Langlands: Monomial Resolutions of Admissible Representations*, World Scientific, 2018. An [author-hosted 2016 draft manuscript](https://victorsnaith.com/AdmobkWSci.pdf) is available; chapters/numbering may differ from final print.

**First-principles problem:** classical Brauer induction decomposes finite-group characters in a Grothendieck group. For smooth admissible representations of locally profinite groups (especially GL_n of a nonarchimedean local field), replace a single character identity by a *chain complex/resolution* of representations induced from one-dimensional characters of certain subgroups. Then use derived-category methods to transport representation-theoretic invariants.

**Original draft structure inspected via its contents:**
1. Finite modulo the Centre Groups — functor categories; bar-monomial resolutions; finiteness in characteristic zero.
2. GL₂ of a Local Field — passage to compact open subgroups; an admissible monomial double complex; explicit GL₂ calculations; descent.
3. Automorphic Representations — GL₂ over the adèles, tensor products, Maass forms, modular forms.
4. GL_n of a Local Field — BN-pairs; Bruhat–Tits buildings; verification of a preceding conjecture under stated conditions.
5. Monomial Resolutions and Deligne Representations — Weil groups and related arithmetic representations.
Other later chapters and appendices need matching to the published edition.

**Key diagram of ideas (not a visualization):**
\[
\text{smooth admissible representations}
\longrightarrow
\text{monomial resolutions}
\longrightarrow
\text{derived-category constructions}
\longrightarrow
\text{candidate arithmetic/automorphic invariants}.
\]
The arrows carry mathematical choices and hypotheses, not just data conversion.

**Terminology:** “monomial” means induced from a character (one-dimensional representation), not a polynomial monomial; “admissible” involves finite-dimensional invariant spaces under compact open subgroups; the “building” is a simplicial structure encoding subgroups and apartments, not a Euclidean building.

**Proof-status caution:** The book develops a framework and constructions, **not** a settled proof of the Langlands program. Victor's later series *Derived Langlands II–VIII* consists of essays/preprints, not additional published books automatically. Later part VI explicitly flags speculative claims; see [other writings](victor-edited-and-other.md).

## Four continuities across the books

1. **Operations and stabilization:** Dyer–Lashof operations → stable splittings → Bott-localized K-theory → revisiting stable homotopy at Arf–Kervaire.
2. **Topological invariants of arithmetic:** characteristic classes of Galois representations → higher K-groups as Galois modules → arithmetic class groups and L-values.
3. **Induction becoming homological:** representation-ring induction in 1994 → monomial resolutions and derived categories in 2018.
4. **Education vs research:** *Groups, Rings and Galois Theory* supplies concrete finite-group and extension calculations needed to approach the technically dense number-theory monographs.

These are **interpretive connections**, not a claim that every chapter follows one historical program.
