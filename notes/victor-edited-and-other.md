# Victor Snaith — edited books, chapters in others' books, unpublished writing

This is a **separate category** from books authored or coauthored by Victor Snaith. Editors are not the authors of every paper in their proceedings, and participation in a conference does not imply authorship of its volume.

## Edited proceedings / research collections

### *Algebraic Topology, Waterloo 1978* (1979)

**Editors:** **Peter Hoffman and Victor Snaith**. Lecture Notes in Mathematics **741**, Springer, 1979 (xi + 654 pp). Proceedings of the Waterloo 1978 conference. [Bibliographic library record](https://ci.nii.ac.jp/author/DA02994968); [Springer search](https://link.springer.com/search?query=Algebraic+Topology+Waterloo+1978).

**Victor's original research contributions in this volume** (obituary bibliography numbers 28 and 30):
- With **Stanley O. Kochman**, “On the stable homotopy of symplectic classifying and Thom spaces,” pp. 394–448.
- With **R. Seymour**, “A J-homomorphism associated with a space of empty varieties (addenda and corrigenda to two papers on the J-homomorphism),” pp. 643–652.

**Area:** homotopy groups, Thom spectra, stable splittings and geometry. This collection complements Victor's standalone 1979 cobordism memoir, but do not merge their bibliographic identities.

### *Current Trends in Algebraic Topology* (1982)

**Editors:** **Richard M. Kane, Stanley O. Kochman, Paul S. Selick, and Victor P. Snaith**. Canadian Mathematical Society Conference Proceedings **2**, two parts, AMS for the CMS, 1982. Proceedings of the 1981 University of Western Ontario meeting. [Library catalogue](https://iucat.iu.edu/iuc/1668518); [bibliography](https://doi.org/10.1112/blms.12802).

**Snaith's chapters in Part 1** (obituary items 40, 43, 45, 46):
- With **Emilio Lluis-Puebla**, “Determination of K₃(F_{p^l}[t]/(t²)) for primes p≥5,” pp. 29–35.
- “A brief survey of Bott periodic algebraic K-theory,” pp. 37–42.
- “A topological proof of a theorem of Ribet,” pp. 43–47.
- With **Haynes Miller**, “On K_*(QRP^n; Z/2),” pp. 233–243.

**Read first:** short Bott-periodic K-theory survey for a period-correct account of his aims; then the algebraic K theory memoir.

### *Algebraic K-Theory: Connections with Geometry and Topology* (1989)

**Editors:** **J. F. Jardine and Victor P. Snaith**. NATO ASI Series C, Mathematical and Physical Sciences **279**, Kluwer, 1989. Proceedings of 1987 Lake Louise school/conference. [Bibliography](https://doi.org/10.1112/blms.12802); [catalog search](https://link.springer.com/search?query=Algebraic+K-Theory+Connections+with+Geometry+and+Topology).

**Victor's chapter:** “Invariants of Representations,” pp. 445–508 (bibliography item 78). Its theme bridges group representation invariants and homotopy/cohomology methods, next to his 1989 Galois-representation monograph. The collection includes other researchers' independent chapters; acknowledge them as authors of their own contributions.

### *Algebraic K-Theory* (1997)

**Editor:** **Victor P. Snaith**. Fields Institute Communications **16**, American Mathematical Society, 1997. Dedicated to the memory of **Robert Wayne Thomason** (1952–1995). [AMS full chapter listing](https://doi.org/10.1090/fic/016).

**Contributions with precise credit:**
- **Ted Chinburg, Manfred Kolster, George Pappas and Victor Snaith**, “Quaternionic exercises in K-theory Galois module structure,” pp. 1–29.
- **Victor Snaith**, “Robert Wayne Thomason, 1952–1995,” pp. ix–xiii (memorial).
- **Victor Snaith**, “Local fundamental classes derived from higher-dimensional K-groups,” pp. 285–323.
- **Victor Snaith**, “Local fundamental classes derived from higher-dimensional K-groups. II,” pp. 325–344.

**Editorial issue:** Some aggregators misleadingly list Thomason as a “coauthor” of the edited book; his role here is the commemorated mathematician. See the volume's title page/front matter before formatting a formal bibliographic citation.

### *Higher Algebraic K-Theory: An Overview* (1992) — contributed, **not edited solely by Snaith**

Lecture Notes in Mathematics **1491**, Springer, 1992. The multi-author volume contains chapters by **Emilio Lluis-Puebla, Jean-Louis Loday, Henri Gillet, Christophe Soulé, and Victor P. Snaith**. [Publisher](https://link.springer.com/search?query=Higher+Algebraic+K-Theory+An+Overview).

**Victor's contribution:** “Applications of Group Cohomology to Bilinear Forms,” pp. 133–164 (obituary item 83).

**Subject:** relate Galois/group cohomology to invariants of symmetric bilinear forms; natural continuation of the methods from *Topological Methods in Galois Representation Theory*. Obtain the text and verify exactly which characteristic classes appear.

## Related authors' works that should not be silently attributed to Snaith

- **Albrecht Fröhlich**, *Galois Module Structure of Algebraic Integers* (a different book, not Victor's 1994 *Galois Module Structure*).
- **Robert Boltje**, “A canonical Brauer induction formula” (1990) and “Monomial resolutions” (2001): crucial predecessors/neighbors, not works authored by Victor.
- **David Gepner and Victor Snaith**, “On the motivic spectra representing algebraic cobordism and algebraic K-theory,” *Documenta Mathematica* **14** (2009), 359–396: joint **article**, not a coauthored book.
- **Michael A. Hill, Michael J. Hopkins and Douglas C. Ravenel**, “On the nonexistence of elements of Kervaire invariant one,” *Annals of Mathematics* **184** (2016): later landmark, distinct from the 2009 Victor monograph.

## Future close-reading bibliography and acknowledgement practice

A bibliography of a contributed volume consists of many researchers' independent work; do not replace their individual names with “Victor Snaith et al.” and do not invent authors if a page is missing.

For each future reading note:
1. Cite *all* authors/editors in the roles they actually occupy and use their publicly used names.
2. Do not infer gender, pronouns or sensitive personal facts, and do not reproduce private addresses from older bibliographies.
3. Identify the actual pages, result/section or reviewed source before describing a theorem as proved.
4. Verify the original bibliography. When taking direct intellectual input from a source cited there, attribute that author by name as well; do not mechanically claim to have studied every cited paper.
